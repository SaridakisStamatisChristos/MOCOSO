## 0. Executive decision: **NO-GO for a novelty-driven algorithms/theory submission at the current evidence state**

The codebase has a valid-looking exact mathematical core and unusually substantial finite verification. The G3 high-modulus algorithm has a credible *parameterized* conditional time bound, and a concrete family separates its behavior from T1's enumerator. G4 and G5 have archived, correctly versioned comparative and numerical results across Ubuntu and Windows. **These are real research assets, not evidence of first-in-literature originality.**

The present central scientific contribution remains insufficiently isolated from established building blocks: general exact subset-sum solution enumeration; convex-subsequence pair/edge-state DP with monotone difference ordering; and single-resource, capacitated longest/shortest path DP on DAGs. A nontrivial bound **relative to a previous implementation** does not on its own constitute an original algorithmic research theorem relative to literature. The paper is not written; the full registered intensive repetition study and an optimized faithful literature baseline are absent. No theorem has been machine-checked or externally refereed. **Do not claim SOTA, first, strongest-known, fundamentally new DP, or publication readiness.**

This is a **revisable** NO-GO: it is not a proof the complete MOCOSO formulation appears in prior literature, nor a recommendation to abandon the work. A technical report or carefully framed application-focused methods paper may be viable with explicit related-work honesty and full disclosure. For an originality-led algorithms paper, G6 needs a demonstrably new structural/algorithmic result or a defensible theory-level noncompositional separation.

## 1. Canonical claim contract

Input: finite array of arbitrary signed integers A[1..n], integer m>=2. A legal selected increasing index tuple I (empty allowed) has selected-index sum divisible by m and every adjacent selected triple obeys a[k]-a[j] > a[j]-a[i]. Maximize number of selected indices, then minimize the **complete index tuple** lexicographically. The proposed T1/G3 specialization requires \`U=n(n+1)/2\`, \`U/2<m<=U\`, \`h=U-m\`, \`q=min(n,h)\`; G3 also sets \`p=min(n,q+2)\`. These regimes are **not** global arbitrary-modulus theorems.

Arithmetic model: all claimed \`O(\cdot)\` time and space are unit-cost **wide-word** RAM bounds whose words can store the input signed integers, their intermediate differences and the DP indices. They are not measured Python wall-time/RSS guarantees for unbounded integers. G2/G3 conditional theorems apply to successful complete execution; cooperative \`RESOURCE_LIMIT\` preflights are not hard-clock/RSS or guaranteed catastrophic OOM recovery.

## 3. Independent prior-art tribunal — sources and exact overlap

Search performed on 2026-10-24 using mechanism descriptions (not just coined MOCOSO acronym): strict convex selected triple, sorted edge difference, subset-sum full enumeration, index-budget/subsequence, exact resource-constrained longest paths on acyclic graphs, capacitated graph DP and fine-grained knapsack algorithms. Consult G1's source catalog for P01–P13, and the following **directly rechecked** originals:

- **Evgeny Kluev (2013), *Longest convex subsequence in an array*, original algorithm disclosure**. https://stackoverflow.com/questions/20664920/longest-convex-subsequence-in-an-array . Specifies sorting candidate pairs by edge-value difference; dynamic subsequence growth in \`O(n² log n)\`; a pair-vertex convex DAG with \`O(n²)\` vertices and \`O(n³)\` edges; back-pointers and a memory-saving witness reconstruction idea. **Overlap:** convexity pair states, ordered-difference sweep, witness reconstruction. **Not equivalent:** no modular selected-index budget or high-modulus deficit.
- **Pranjal Dutta and Mahesh Sreekumar Rajasree, *Efficient Reductions and Algorithms for Variants of Subset Sum* (arXiv 2112.11020, 2021/2022 extended preprint)**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.11020 . The longer text, Appendix B.1, uses reachability Bellman DP and predecessor DFS to enumerate exact-target subsets. **Overlap:** T1's predecessor-pruned omission enumeration and standard leaf-charging principle. **Not equivalent:** arbitrary positive weights, no MOCOSO strict-convex objective; its all-solutions storage is not T1 streaming-space.
- **Eduardo Camponogara and Ricardo Boveto Shima (2010), *Mobile Agent Routing with Time Constraints: A Resource Constrained Longest-Path Approach***. https://doi.org/10.3217/jucs-016-03-0372 . Authors formulate a constrained longest path in a DAG and use pseudopolynomial DP. **Overlap:** resource-state optimization on directed acyclic paths. **Not equivalent:** different application/objective/lex constraints, no established MOCOSO-specific p/h bound.
- **Kyriakos Axiotis and Christos Tzamos, *Capacitated Dynamic Programming: Faster Knapsack and Graph Algorithms*, ICALP 2019**. https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ICALP.2019.19 . Independently located current closest *general capacitated-graph algorithms* source not previously centered by G1/G3. Explicitly studies a weight-budgeted optimal path in edge/node-weighted DAGs and discusses \`O(k|E|)\` generic DP, then improved parameter dependence for structured instances. **Overlap:** the general DP-with-resource paradigm in which G3's pair-state/resource DP belongs. **Not equivalent established:** its theorem **does not, from the source inspected, supply a literal O(n+p²(h+1)) strict-convex MOCOSO result** or the exact lex witness. Further theorem-level head-to-head is mandatory before priority claims.
- **Ilyas Himmich, Issmail El Hallaoui, François Soumis, *A multiphase dynamic programming algorithm for the shortest path problem with resource constraints* (European Journal of Operational Research 315(2), 2024)**. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0377221723009050 . A primary publisher abstract for a more recent exact resource-path DP. **Overlap:** substantial evolving prior methods; *not* claimed to solve MOCOSO. This broadens the required competitive benchmark search, not an excuse to pretend algorithmic equivalence.
- **Classical distinct-part partitions**: \`P_d(h)\` is a known generating-function coefficient; the product/partition bound is not a new theorem, irrespective of whether MOCOSO's application is novel. https://oeis.org/A000009 .

**Novelty-by-contribution:** target formulation **LIKELY DISTINCT WITHIN THIS NONEXHAUSTIVE SEARCH**, exact q+2/q+3 locality **potentially application-specific**, T1 search technique **SUBSTANTIALLY OVERLAPPING**, G3 capacitated DP **INCREMENTAL/COMBINATIONAL**, output-sensitive vs pseudopolynomial separation **new observation for these two local implementations, not a literature breakthrough**, strong general SOTA **UNRESOLVED**. The literature search does not establish global negative novelty or uniqueness.

### Strongest collapse test — explicit reduction

The convexity predicate requires only two previously selected indices, so represent any selected local prefix by a pair-state vertex (i,j). An admissible transition (i,j)→(j,k) exists when i<j<k and \`a_k>2a_j-a_i\`. Attach resource weight \`g(j,k)\` equal to the sum of omitted local indices between successive selections, and a unit reward for each selected k. Enforce the compulsory suffix and terminal omissions via source/sink costs. This produces a **single-resource constrained longest-path problem on an acyclic pair graph**, which supports the known generic DP at cost \`O(|E|(h+1)) = O(p³(h+1))\` in the naive edge-explicit model.

G3 improves this **naive** bound to O(p²(h+1)) by sweeping monotone value thresholds for each fixed second endpoint j while caching best residue/omission-cost continuations. But the **threshold monotonicity** is exactly the structure exploited by the earlier unconstrained convex-edge methods. Without a proof that this combination yields a genuinely new asymptotic boundary **relative to the appropriate structurally optimized constrained-path/sorted-edge prior algorithms**, the proposed algorithmic originality is not demonstrated. It can still be a correct, useful, and instructive specialization.

**Do not conflate:** “the known generic DAG routine is O(p³h)” with “every prior algorithm necessarily needs Ω(p³h).” The latter lower bound has not been established.

## 4. Formal/mathematical adversarial review

1. **Complement correctness:** rederived. Requires m>U/2, nonempty selection; empty solution remains legal even when no nonempty modular selection exists.
2. **Fixed suffix:** check all ordinary triples ending at k>=q+3, and all selected triples ending <=q+2 inside [1,p]. The crossing-boundary off-by-one issue was appropriately addressed; no counterexample identified in the reviewed proof.
3. **G3 recurrence:** for two selected local endpoints, the omitted-gap weights partition omissions into before first, between pairs, after last; transitions enforce strict difference and mandatory suffix. Descending second-index order establishes acyclicity. Sorting predecessor values increasingly makes threshold \`2a_j-a_i\` nonincreasing; successor values descending permits each successor processed once per j for every cost. With length and smallest-next-index tie pointers, the exact complete tuple lex criterion follows by suffix induction. **Conditional human mathematical validation**, not formal verification or an independent exhaustive global proof of Python code.
4. **Explicit work/space:** count O(p²) pair rows and at most h+1 cells per row, O(p²h) successor/copy work plus O(n) initial scan and final witness. Temporary row/cost arrays and dictionary/table pointers counted in abstract word model. Python object memory and big-integer width disallow a universal fixed-word runtime/RSS promise. No hard memory or wall-time cutoff is proved.
5. **T1/G3 asymptotic comparison:** choose n=h+1, m=U-h, signed positive \`a_i=3^{i-1}\`. All subsequences are strictly convex; the T1 enumeration in optimization mode visits all distinct omission partitions of sum h (P_d(h)), whereas G3's work is polynomial in h. This separates the two specific **implementations**. It does **not** prove novelty over all past algorithms, lower bounds for MOCOSO or a standard fixed-word complexity result for arbitrary huge integer values.
6. **Adversarial deficits:** n=2 singleton; h=0; q=n; negative huge input integers; equal adjacent differences; crossing triples; lex ties; partial \`RESOURCE_LIMIT\` must not imply mathematical INFEASIBLE. The G2/G3 independent finite suites and G4 generic-DAG oracle cover many cases; harder non-superconvex large positive-optimum and externally generated counterexample suites remain useful.
7. **Formal proof status:** no Lean/Coq/Isabelle checker and no theorem proof formally verified; strong standalone theorem submission needs external human proof scrutiny and ideally executable formal/SMT aid where tractable. Lack of a checker is *not by itself* a logical refutation.

No decisive mathematical counterexample was identified in the material inspected. A correctness PASS does not transfer to a novelty PASS.

More diverse *positive-optimum* random signed/hard cases and hardware repetition required for a comparative methods claim. Linux process RSS vs tracemalloc instrumentation have different scopes.

## 9. Ordered, falsifiable remediation plan

**P0 — Novelty boundary (mandatory before writing a theory paper).** Produce a line-by-line theorem-mechanism comparison of G3 recurrence vs both sorted-edge convex subsequence baseline (2013 disclosure) and general capacitated DAG DP (ICALP 2019), including resources, graph size, strict inequality, full-lex witness, suffix kernel and unit word-bit model. Independently investigate related journal/algorithm papers through citation chains. If it follows, **withdraw strongest novelty claim** and decide whether to publish a methods/educational note instead.

**P1 — Genuine new result (required for higher-selectivity theoretical venue).** Attempt a structural kernel/lex-space tradeoff not implied by standard resource-DAG and convex edge sweeps; plausible *research targets* (not promised results): time \`O(n+h² polylog h)\` or space \`O(h²)\` for exact full-lex optimality, or a nontrivial lower bound/structural characterization. A theorem must include proof, adversarial counterexamples, and a fair model. Failure to improve is a valid negative research result, not license for strong claims.

**P2 — Proper baselines and hard-case benchmarks.** Implement/derive a competitive, faithful **optimized** resource-constrained pair-DAG DP with the same full-lex semantics, not only tuple-copied generic DP; add strict sorted-edge+index-budget baseline. Include n/h parameter grid, many positive-optimum signed distributions, equal/tied differences, non-superconvex crossing-boundary cases, lex collisions, near resource caps, and memory-tight regimes. Report all skips, losses, statuses, peak traced allocations vs process RSS separately, full tuple comparisons and true hardware/environment.

**P3 — Statistical/reproducibility maturity.** Execute proposed 146-case paper profile at original **3 blocks×5 untraced repetitions**, ideally across >1 physical machine per OS. Pre-register changes/metrics before measuring; preserve per-case/block raw JSON and SHA256, report robust distribution/uncertainty rather than a single cherry-picked speedup. G5 source-pin/archival is already useful and should remain untouched.

**P4 — Independent proof challenge.** A distinct mathematical reviewer should inspect the exact pair-DAG equivalence, omitted-gap accounting, word/bit complexity, lex pointer induction, empty/singleton edge cases and resource return semantics; optionally formalize main theorem/invariants with an actually executed checker (not just generated proof syntax). **P5 — Manuscript only after contribution threshold.** Write a paper with mathematically precise theorem statements, mechanism-level related work and primary citations, falsifiers/limitations, raw evidence manifest, source SHA, no unearned priority labels, and venue-specific policies checked from official sources. If P0–P1 cannot establish theory-level novelty, explicitly choose an application/methods/technical-report framing, not a “new SOTA algorithm” title.
